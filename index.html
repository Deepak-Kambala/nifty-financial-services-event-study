What happened to the NIFTY Financial Services Index around the Union Budget and RBI MPC announcements?

Deepak Kambala
JNTUK University College of Engineering Narasaraopet

Question

This study examines whether six policy announcements were followed by returns that differed from the index's recent average return. The index is a sectoral benchmark of the 20 largest NSE-listed financial-services companies by free-float market capitalisation.

It reports the return on the announcement day, the cumulative abnormal return over 11 trading days, and the change in return volatility. The largest reaction was on the Union Budget 2026 event day: abnormal return −2.264%. The study's second test reports t = −4.159 and p = 0.0003 for that observation. This is evidence of an unusually large movement relative to the study's baseline; it does not by itself prove that the announcement caused the entire move.

Data and events

The dataset contains daily open, high, low, close, volume and turnover fields for the NIFTY Financial Services Index. The paper describes the sample as 31 March 2025 to 31 March 2026 with 247 trading days; the CSV and notebook data run from 1 April 2025 to 30 March 2026. Returns are arithmetic percentage changes in the closing level.

Method

The design follows the standard event-study framework described by Brown and Warner (1985) and MacKinlay (1997). It uses a mean-adjusted return model: the expected return during an event window is the index's mean return in a preceding estimation window. No separate market benchmark is used.

Event day and windows

t_0 = \min\{\, t \in \mathcal{T} \mid t \ge t_{\text{announced}} \,\}
W = \{t : t_0-5 \le t \le t_0+5\}, \qquad L = \{t : t_0-35 \le t \le t_0-6\}

\mathcal{T} is the set of trading days in the dataset and t_{\text{announced}} the official date. The rule prevents the event day from preceding the release.

Abnormal and cumulative abnormal returns

R_t = \frac{\text{Close}_t - \text{Close}_{t-1}}{\text{Close}_{t-1}} \times 100, \qquad \bar R_i = \frac{1}{30}\sum_{t\in L} R_{it}
AR_{it} = R_{it} - \bar R_i, \qquad CAR_i(-5,+5) = \sum_{t=-5}^{+5} AR_{it}

A negative AR_{it} means the index returned less than its estimation-window average. CAR_i is the sum across all 11 trading days, including the five days before the event.

Volatility

\sigma_{i,\text{pre}} = \operatorname{std}\{R_t : t\in[t_0-35,\,t_0-6]\}, \quad \sigma_{i,\text{post}} = \operatorname{std}\{R_t : t\in[t_0,\,t_0+5]\}, \quad \Delta\sigma_i = \sigma_{i,\text{post}}-\sigma_{i,\text{pre}}

The post-event volatility uses the event day and the following five trading days. The paper also plots a 30-day rolling standard deviation for the full sample.

Significance tests

Test 1 is a one-sample t-test of whether the mean of the 11 window abnormal returns is zero (10 degrees of freedom). Test 2 is described in the paper as a Patell standardised event-day test. The notebook calculates the event-day abnormal return divided by the estimation-window standard deviation:

t_2 = \frac{AR_{i0}}{\sigma_{i,\text{pre}}}, \qquad p = 2\,P(T_{29} > |t_2|)

It then uses a two-sided Student's t reference distribution with 29 degrees of freedom. For the Budget, −2.264 / 0.544 is approximately −4.16. This is the statistic implemented in the notebook; it should not be read as a full market-model causal test.

On Test 2, the Budget (p = 0.0003) and October 2025 MPC (p = 0.0401) are statistically significant at the 5% level; only the Budget meets the 1% threshold. Test 1 is not significant for any event. Budget volatility rises from 0.544% before the event to 1.798% after it, a change of +1.253 percentage points. Its full-window CAR is +3.015% despite the −2.264% event-day abnormal return, meaning the other days in the 11-day window more than offset the event-day decline.

Reading the results

These are direct readings of the reported numbers, not claims that the study tested a particular news explanation.

The two February 2026 windows overlap

The Budget event (1 February) and the February MPC decision (6 February) are close together. Their 11-day windows therefore overlap. The reported CARs for these two events should not be treated as independent pieces of evidence.

Event-day significance and window significance disagree

For the Budget, Test 2 is significant (p = 0.0003), while Test 1 is not (p = 0.5374). The tests answer different questions: whether the event-day abnormal return was unusual relative to the estimation-window variation, and whether the mean abnormal return across the full 11-day window differed from zero.

Scope

  • Six events and one index. The paper itself attributes non-significant results to low power.
  • Twelve tests are reported (two per event) with no multiple-comparison adjustment described; the October 2025 result at p = 0.0401 is the weaker of the two rejections.
  • The mean-adjusted model has no market benchmark, so broad market moves in the window are not removed from the abnormal returns.
  • The study does not measure investor expectations, the surprise component of an announcement, or the independent effect of other news on the same days.

Notes on the source

For transparency, the paper and the executable notebook describe the inputs and method slightly differently in places:

  • Sample period. Paper: 31 Mar 2025 to 31 Mar 2026. Notebook output: 1 Apr 2025 to 30 Mar 2026, 247 trading days.
  • Event count. The paper says seven relevant events were identified and six were analysed. The notebook's event dictionary also includes the February 2025 Union Budget, which is outside the sample and produces no usable result.
  • Test 2 description. The paper labels Test 2 a Patell test. The notebook implementation is the event-day abnormal return divided by the estimation-window standard deviation, followed by a two-sided t p-value.
  • Rounding. The Budget's pre-event volatility appears as 0.544% in the results table and about 0.545% in the discussion; the difference is rounding.
  • Interpretation. The paper discusses possible explanations for the Budget reaction, but the event study does not separately test those explanations.
