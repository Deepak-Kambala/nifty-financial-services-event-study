An Event Study of Abnormal Returns and Volatility Patterns in India's NIFTY Financial Services Index Around Union Budget and RBI Monetary Policy Committee Announcements (FY2026)

Deepak Kambala
JNTUK University College of Engineering Narasaraopet

Question

The paper asks whether the Union Budget and Reserve Bank of India Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) announcements produced statistically significant abnormal returns in the NIFTY Financial Services Index during FY2026. The index tracks the 20 largest NSE-listed financial companies by free-float market capitalisation, so its constituents' revenues (interest spreads, treasury gains, insurance premiums, capital-market activity) depend directly on rate and tax decisions.

The study measures three things around six dated events: the event-day abnormal return, the cumulative abnormal return over an 11-day window, and the change in return volatility. Its headline result is that the Union Budget on 1 February 2026 was followed by an event-day abnormal return of −2.264% and a post-event volatility of 1.798% against a pre-event 0.544%. It was the only event significant at the 1% level.

Data and events

The data are daily OHLCV records for the index from the NSE historical data portal: 247 trading days. The notebook reports the span as 1 April 2025 to 30 March 2026; the paper states 31 March 2025 to 31 March 2026 (see Notes). Returns are arithmetic daily percentage changes in the closing level.

Method

The design follows the standard event study of Brown and Warner (1985) and MacKinlay (1997), with a mean-adjusted return model: the expected return on each event-window day is the mean return over a preceding estimation window. No market-index benchmark is used.

Event day and windows

t_0 = \min\{\, t \in \mathcal{T} \mid t \ge t_{\text{announced}} \,\}
W = \{t : t_0-5 \le t \le t_0+5\}, \qquad L = \{t : t_0-35 \le t \le t_0-6\}

\mathcal{T} is the set of trading days in the dataset and t_{\text{announced}} the official date. The rule prevents the event day from preceding the release.

Abnormal and cumulative abnormal returns

R_t = \frac{\text{Close}_t - \text{Close}_{t-1}}{\text{Close}_{t-1}} \times 100, \qquad \bar R_i = \frac{1}{30}\sum_{t\in L} R_{it}
AR_{it} = R_{it} - \bar R_i, \qquad CAR_i(-5,+5) = \sum_{t=-5}^{+5} AR_{it}

A negative AR_{it} means the index returned less than its own average over the estimation window. CAR_i sums all eleven days, including the five before the event.

Volatility

\sigma_{i,\text{pre}} = \operatorname{std}\{R_t : t\in[t_0-35,\,t_0-6]\}, \quad \sigma_{i,\text{post}} = \operatorname{std}\{R_t : t\in[t_0,\,t_0+5]\}, \quad \Delta\sigma_i = \sigma_{i,\text{post}}-\sigma_{i,\text{pre}}

The post-event window has six observations because it includes the event day. The paper also plots a 30-day rolling standard deviation for the whole sample.

Significance tests

Test 1 is a one-sample t-test of whether the mean of the 11 window ARs is zero (10 degrees of freedom). Test 2, which the paper calls the Patell test, standardises the event-day abnormal return by the estimation-window standard deviation. In the notebook it is

t_2 = \frac{AR_{i0}}{\sigma_{i,\text{pre}}}, \qquad p = 2\,P(T_{29} > |t_2|)

with a Student-t reference distribution on n_{\text{est}}-1 = 29 degrees of freedom. This reproduces the paper's table: for the Budget, −2.264 / 0.544 ≈ −4.16.

Test 2 gives two rejections of a zero event-day abnormal return: the Budget (p = 0.0003) and the October 2025 MPC (p = 0.0401). Test 1 rejects for none of the six events. The Budget's post-event volatility is 1.798% against 0.544% before, a ratio of about 3.3, and its Δσ of +1.253 is the largest by a wide margin; the next largest is +0.095 (December 2025 MPC). The paper's Budget CAR of +3.015% is positive even though the event-day abnormal return is −2.264%, so the window's net movement comes from the other ten days.

Reading the results

The following points are the explainer's analysis, derived from the paper's definitions and tables rather than stated by the paper.

The two February 2026 windows overlap

The Budget (1 February) and the February MPC decision (6 February) are five trading days apart. The Budget's days t₀ to t₀+5 are therefore the MPC's days t₀−5 to t₀, and the Budget day itself sits inside the MPC's event window. The two CARs, +3.015% and +3.018%, therefore share six of their eleven daily terms. Figure 6.1 of the paper agrees: its February MPC row shows an abnormal return of about −2.31% on D−5, which is the Budget day's return. The two events are not independent observations.

Event-day significance and window significance disagree

For the Budget, Test 2 is strongly significant and Test 1 is not (p = 0.54). Test 1 averages eleven daily ARs of both signs, so a single large negative day can be offset by later positive days. The two tests answer different questions: whether event day was unusual, and whether the window as a whole departed from zero.

Scope

  • Six events and one index. The paper itself attributes non-significant results to low power.
  • Twelve tests are reported (two per event) with no multiple-comparison adjustment described; the October 2025 result at p = 0.0401 is the weaker of the two rejections.
  • The mean-adjusted model has no market benchmark, so broad market moves in the window are not removed from the abnormal returns.
  • The paper notes that the size of a reaction depends on the information surprise, but it does not measure expectations or surprise. Its explanations for the Budget reaction (deficit target, capital-gains changes, banking-sector measures) are offered as possibilities and are not tested.

Notes on the source

Comparing the paper with the accompanying notebook shows these inconsistencies. They do not change the reported numbers, which the notebook reproduces.

  • Sample period. Paper: 31 Mar 2025 to 31 Mar 2026. Notebook output: 1 Apr 2025 to 30 Mar 2026, 247 trading days.
  • Window gap. The text describes a one-day separation between estimation and event windows. The equations (L ends at t₀−6, W starts at t₀−5) and the code leave none.
  • Test 2. The paper calls it the Patell test. The implemented statistic is AR₀ divided by σpre, without a prediction-error adjustment; a comment in the code gives a different formula (σ/√n) that is not the one executed.
  • Event count. The paper says seven events were identified. The notebook defines eight, including a Union Budget dated 1 Feb 2025 that precedes the data; it and the April 2025 MPC are dropped for lack of estimation data.
  • Rounding. The text gives the Budget's pre-event σ as 0.545% in one place and 0.544% in the table; the notebook value is 0.5445%.
  • Volatility wording. The abstract's "near tripling" understates the ratio of about 3.3.
